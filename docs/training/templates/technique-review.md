## Filming

- Film a normal, comfortable working set; do not lift heavier to make a review
  possible. Include setup, unracking / walkout if relevant, all reps and reracking.
- Use a stable camera with the whole body, bar and plates visible; avoid wide
  lens distortion and a moving camera. Side and an oblique view are useful;
  one angle cannot establish every aspect of a movement.
- Note exercise/variant, date, load, reps, approximate effort, belt/equipment and
  any sensation. State whether it is an old pre-injury clip or current footage.
- Keep original playback speed. Slow-motion copies can supplement the original.
- Store personal media/reviews under `.local/training/technique/` unless the owner
  chooses another private location. Do not commit identifiable gym footage.

Review movement consistency, balance, bar/body relationship and control in the
context of the chosen lift, anatomy and goal. Avoid imposing a single ideal
torso angle or declaring sumo inherently safe/unsafe. Visible technique does not
reveal a disc diagnosis, internal tissue stress or a guaranteed injury cause.

## Pattern status

- `unconfirmed`: a single ambiguous observation.
- `observed_once`: clearly visible in one clip, with timestamp.
- `repeated`: supported across identified reps/sets or later clips.
- `improving`: comparable later footage supports a change.
- `not_visible`: camera/occlusion prevents an assessment.
- `resolved_or_not_reproduced`: state the comparison; keep earlier observations.

Start with one or two relevant, testable cues. A cue that increases symptoms or
reduces control is not a successful correction. Record the next attempt and
the owner's response; do not keep stacking contradictory cues.
